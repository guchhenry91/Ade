### [SECTION 1 — Why Credit Matters — 0:30 to 1:30]
**[SCREEN: Show examples — apartment application, car loan interest rates, credit card approval]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Before we get into the steps, let's talk about why credit even matters.

Your credit score is basically a trust score. Lenders, landlords, even some employers use it to decide if they want to work with you.

A good credit score means:
- Lower interest rates on loans and car payments
- Easier approval for apartments
- Access to better credit cards with real rewards

A bad score — or no score at all — means higher rates, more rejections, and paying more for the same things everyone else gets cheaper.

So building credit isn't just about having a credit card. It's about giving yourself options."

### [SECTION 2 — Why You Have No Credit — 1:30 to 2:45]
**[SCREEN: Animated graphic showing the "credit catch-22" — need credit to get credit]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Here's the frustrating part nobody talks about.

To get credit, you usually need a credit history. But to have a credit history, you need credit. It's a catch-22.

This is exactly why so many people feel stuck.

You apply for a credit card, you get denied. You try again, another denial. And every time you apply, it dings your score — even though you don't have one yet.

The key is knowing which products are actually designed for people with no credit. That's what I'm going to show you."

### [SECTION 3 — Step 1: Check Your Starting Point — 2:45 to 4:00]
**[SCREEN RECORDING: Open Credit Karma or Experian — show the free credit check process]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Step one — before you do anything else, check where you actually stand.

Go to Credit Karma dot com or the Experian app — both are completely free, no credit card required.

[SCREEN: Navigate to Credit Karma, show the score dashboard]

When you check, one of two things will happen:

Option A — you'll see a score, probably between 500 and 620. This means you have some history already — maybe a student loan or an old account.

Option B — it says 'No Score' or 'Thin File.' This means you're truly starting from zero.

Either way, that's your baseline. Write it down. You're going to watch it grow."

---

### [SECTION 4 — Step 2: Get a Secured Credit Card — 4:00 to 5:30]
**[SCREEN RECORDING: Show Discover it Secured or Capital One Secured card page]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Step two — get a secured credit card. This is the fastest, most reliable way to start building credit.

Here's how it works: you put down a deposit — usually $200 — and that deposit becomes your credit limit. The bank isn't taking a risk on you because you already put up the money.

But here's the important part — you use the card like a normal credit card, and the bank reports your payments to all three credit bureaus every month. That's what builds your score.

[SCREEN: Show Discover it Secured card details]

The two cards I recommend for beginners:

Number one — Discover it Secured. No annual fee, earns cash back, and Discover will automatically upgrade you to an unsecured card after 7 months of on-time payments.

Number two — Capital One Platinum Secured. Low deposit options, starts at $49, and Capital One is well known for graduating people quickly.

The rule: use the card for one small purchase per month — like a Netflix subscription or gas. Then pay it off IN FULL every month. That's it."

---

### [SECTION 5 — Step 3: Become an Authorized User — 5:30 to 6:45]
**[SCREEN: Simple graphic showing "Authorized User" concept]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Step three — become an authorized user on someone else's credit card.

This is one of the most underrated credit-building moves, and most people don't know about it.

Here's how it works: if you have a family member or close friend with a credit card in good standing — meaning they pay on time and keep the balance low — they can add you as an authorized user on their account.

Once they do, that card's entire history shows up on YOUR credit report. If they've had the card for 5 years with no missed payments, you instantly get credit for 5 years of good history.

You don't even have to use the card. You don't need to have the physical card in your hand. Just being listed as an authorized user is enough.

Important: make sure the person you ask has good habits. If they're carrying a high balance or missing payments, it will hurt your score, not help it."

---

### [SECTION 6 — Step 4: Get a Credit Builder Loan — 6:45 to 8:00]
**[SCREEN RECORDING: Show Self.inc website or a local credit union page]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Step four — a credit builder loan.

This one is different from a regular loan. With a credit builder loan, you don't get the money upfront. Instead, you make small monthly payments — usually $25 to $50 — and at the end of the term, you get the full amount back.

The whole point is to create a track record of on-time payments, which gets reported to the credit bureaus every month.

[SCREEN: Show Self.inc dashboard]

Self dot inc is the most popular option. You can start with as little as $25 a month, and it reports to all three bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

Credit unions also offer these. If you have a local credit union, call them and ask about a credit builder loan. The rates are usually better.

Combined with your secured card, this gives you two separate accounts reporting every month — which speeds up your progress significantly."

---

### [SECTION 7 — Step 5: The Golden Rules — 8:00 to 9:30]
**[SCREEN: Animated checklist appearing one by one]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"Now that you have the tools in place, here are the rules you absolutely cannot break.

[SCREEN TEXT: Rule 1 — Always Pay On Time]
Rule one: Pay on time, every single time. Payment history is 35% of your score. One missed payment can drop your score by 60 to 100 points. Set up autopay for at least the minimum payment so you never miss.

[SCREEN TEXT: Rule 2 — Keep Utilization Below 10%]
Rule two: Keep your credit utilization below 10%. If your secured card has a $200 limit, never carry more than $20 on it at a time. Utilization is 30% of your score — this one matters more than most people realize.

[SCREEN TEXT: Rule 3 — Don't Apply for Too Much]
Rule three: Don't apply for multiple credit cards at once. Every application triggers a hard inquiry, which temporarily drops your score. Apply for one thing, let it age, then consider the next step.

[SCREEN TEXT: Rule 4 — Let Accounts Age]
Rule four: Don't close your first credit card. The age of your accounts matters. Keep that first secured card open even after you get better cards."

### [SECTION 8 — How Fast Can You See Results? — 9:30 to 10:30]
**[SCREEN: Simple timeline graphic — 30 days, 60 days, 6 months, 1 year]**

**VOICEOVER:**
"So how fast does this actually work?

Here's a realistic timeline:

30 days — Your secured card and credit builder loan report for the first time. You may start seeing a score appear if you had none before.

60 days — Score is typically in the 580 to 620 range. You're officially in the system.

6 months — With on-time payments and low utilization, most people are in the 650 to 680 range. This is where real doors start opening.

12 months — 700 or above is very achievable. At this point you can apply for unsecured cards with real rewards, better apartment approvals, and lower loan rates.

The process isn't complicated. It just takes consistency."
